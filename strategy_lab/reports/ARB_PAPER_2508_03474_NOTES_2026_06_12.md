## 10-Bullet Summary

1. **Two arb types**: Market Rebalancing Arbitrage (intra-market, binary or multi-condition) vs Combinatorial Arbitrage (inter-market, dependent market pairs). The binary crypto up/down case = Market Rebalancing Arbitrage, the simplest form.
2. **Formal gate**: Long arb when `sum(YES_i) < 1`; Short arb when `sum(YES_i) > 1`. For binary (2-condition): long = `p_UP + p_DN < 1`; short = `p_UP + p_DN > 1`. Profit is `|sum − 1|` per unit. Confirms our `sum_ask < 1` framework is correct.
3. **Execution is NON-ATOMIC**: CLOB order-by-order matching means leg 2 can fail after leg 1 fills. No atomic bundling. This is the key execution risk vs DeFi AMM arb.
4. **Split/Merge/Redeem**: Split = lock 1 USDC → mint 1 YES + 1 NO (PositionSplit event). Merge = burn 1 YES + 1 NO → recover 1 USDC when both selling sides sum to $1 (OrdersMerged event). Redeem = resolution payout at $1 for winning token. **No per-trade fee** (confirmed: paper states Polymarket does not charge per trade executed).
5. **$39.6M total extracted** (ε=$1 filter), dominated by intra-market strategies. Long buy-side extraction ($11.1M buying YES + $17.3M buying NO in multi-condition markets) far exceeds short (sell-side). Crypto has the biggest outlier opportunities per condition.
6. **~1% of election opportunities exploited**: Most arb goes uncaptured, especially U.S. election (Nov 2024). Sports single-condition markets are most aggressively exploited in dollar terms, surpassing election in total realized profit.
7. **Extreme concentration at top**: Top 1 wallet = $2.0M, top 10 collectively hold the majority. Ranks 4 and 9 have only 211 and 200 transactions but $768k/$424k respectively — almost certainly taker sweepers hitting large single opportunities rather than ladders.
8. **Short-window crypto markets NOT separately studied**: Paper covers Polymarket broadly (politics, sports, crypto) but does not isolate 5m/15m up/down market type. Crypto topic has largest outlier yield-per-dollar opportunities, confirming our domain has persistent mispricing.
9. **Detection threshold = 2 cents**: Paper filters to |sum − 1| > $0.02 on the dollar minimum. Our b945's median sum_ask observed at ~1.02-1.04 suggests the paper would capture our market type.
10. **No sizing formula**: Paper computes realized profit by min-tokens-held × (1 − entry_cost), but provides no optimal sizing or EV formula. Fees not modeled (stated as zero, which matched Polymarket at measurement time; our 0.07 × p × (1−p) curve was later introduced).

## 1. Taxonomy + Formal Conditions

### 1.1 Market Rebalancing Arbitrage (Intra-Market)

**Definition 3** (paper notation, translated):

Let M be a market with conditions {C₁, ..., Cₙ}. Let `val(Yᵢ, t)` = price of YES for condition Cᵢ at time t.

**Long MRA** (buy-side arb, our sum_ask < 1): Opportunity exists at t if:
```
∑ᵢ val(Yᵢ, t) < 1
```
Guaranteed profit = `1 − ∑ᵢ val(Yᵢ, t)` per unit bought across all conditions.

**Short MRA** (sell-side arb, sum_bid > 1): Opportunity exists at t if:
```
∑ᵢ val(Yᵢ, t) > 1
```
Since YES sum > 1 implies NO tokens are undervalued. Buying all NO tokens, guaranteed profit:
```
n − ∑ᵢ val(Nᵢ, t) = ∑ᵢ val(Yᵢ, t) − 1
```
where `val(Nᵢ, t)` = NO price for Cᵢ, and sum of all NO resolutions = n − 1.

**Alternative Short strategy**: Buy 1 USDC as Split for each condition (mint YES+NO), then sell YES immediately. Profit = `∑ᵢ val(Yᵢ, t) − 1`.

**Our terms translation**:
- `val(Y_UP, t) + val(Y_DN, t)` = `sum_ask` (when filled at ask)
- Long MRA = `sum_ask < 1.0` → b945/ce25 strategy
- Short MRA = `sum_bid > 1.0` → sell both YES positions via Split+sell

**Key footnote (Definition 3, fn 6)**: For a single binary condition, treat YES and NO as two conditions. Arb when `p_YES + p_NO < 1` (buy both) or `p_YES + p_NO > 1` (Split+sell both). This is EXACTLY the crypto up/down binary case.

### 1.2 Combinatorial Arbitrage (Inter-Market)

**Definition 4** (paper notation):

Let M1, M2 be dependent markets with dependent subsets S ⊂ M1 and S' ⊂ M2.

Arb exists at t if either:
1. `∑_{c∈S} val(T_c, t) < ∑_{c'∈S'} val(T_{c'}, t)` → hold YES for S, hold YES for complement of S'
2. `∑_{c∈S} val(T_c, t) > ∑_{c'∈S'} val(T_{c'}, t)` → hold YES for complement of S, hold YES for S'

Note: Complement of S in M = M − S = all conditions NOT in S = equivalent to holding all NO for S.

Profit = `|∑_{c∈S} val(T_c, t) − ∑_{c'∈S'} val(T_{c'}, t)|`

Paper notes: "NO" positions not considered for Combinatorial because multiple NOs can be true simultaneously (NegRisk multi-outcome), breaking the symmetry.

**Practical scope**: Only 13 valid Combinatorial Arb market pairs found in the entire dataset (all US 2024 election related: state winner vs margin markets, presidency+senate combos, etc.). **Crypto up/down markets are NOT NegRisk and not combinatorially related to other markets** — pure Market Rebalancing Arbitrage domain.

## 2. CTF Mechanics (Split/Merge/Redeem)

### On-Chain Events

| Event | Trigger | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|
| `PositionSplit` | User locks 1 USDC → receives 1 YES + 1 NO | Split operation |
| `OrderFilled` × 2 | On any matched buy/sell | Standard CLOB fill |
| `OrdersMerged` | Two opposing orders sum to $1.00 OR user sells both positions | Merge: burns YES+NO, returns USDC |
| Resolution redeem | Market resolves, UMA oracle confirms | Winner token redeemed at $1.00 |

**Merge mechanism** (important): When a YES bid at $0.60 matches NO bid at $0.40, $1.00 is locked → 1 YES + 1 NO minted (PositionSplit + 2× OrderFilled). When selling both positions summing to $1.00 → tokens burned, USDC returned (OrdersMerged).

**NegRisk adapter / CONVERSION**: NegRisk markets have a shared market ID. Conditions within NegRisk share a `neg_risk_market_id`. The CONVERSION event in Polymarket API = NegRisk YES+NO → USDC conversion via the NegRisk adapter. Paper does not detail the gas cost, but treats it as a conversion mechanism not a trade.

**Gasless transactions**: Paper does not address gas. Polymarket uses gasless order submission (Tier-1 relayer system). On-chain settlement is batched by Polymarket operators. No per-trade gas cost to arbitrageurs in normal operation.

### Arb via Split for Short MRA

Short MRA execution path:
1. Lock N USDC per condition → PositionSplit → get 1 YES + 1 NO per condition per USDC
2. Immediately sell all YES positions (n sell orders) at market — profit is `∑ val(Yᵢ) − 1` per unit
3. Keep all NO tokens; when market resolves, exactly one resolves at $1, rest at $0 → net $1 per unit locked

**Risk**: Steps 2 are non-atomic. If any YES leg fails to sell at quoted price, locked USDC is stranded until market resolution (or until you can exit via secondary sale).

## 3. Empirical Findings

### Arb Detection Methodology (Section 6)

- **Price computation**: VWAP over executed bids per block (≈ 2 seconds). Forward-carry last price up to 5,000 blocks (≈ 2.5 hours) if no trade; set to 0 if gap exceeds that.
- **Detection threshold**: Only report opportunities where `|1 − VWAP_sum| > $0.02` on the dollar.
- **Liquidity filter**: Exclude conditions where any token trades above $0.95 (> 95% probability resolved — near-certain markets excluded).
- **Window for grouping**: T = 950 blocks ≈ 1 hour. Bids within T are considered the same opportunity.

### Market-Level Frequency (Section 6.1 — single conditions)

- Of 8,659 single-condition markets: many had opportunities, **Crypto has biggest outliers** per condition.
- Median profit per dollar across ALL topics ≈ **$0.60 on the dollar** (not 2 cents) — showing "remarkable market inefficiency". This is the max available profit at any moment, not the realized profit.
- Average max opportunity profit (at $100 budget) ≈ **$100 average per condition** → typical token volume < 2K.

### NegRisk (multi-condition) Arb (Section 6.2)

- Of 1,578 NegRisk markets: **662 had at least one arb opportunity** (42%).
- Average max profit per opportunity: **~$0.40 on the dollar** for both long and short.
- Sports dominates in number of opportunities; Politics dominates in dollar value (US election).

### Crypto-Specific Observation

- Crypto topic has **largest outlier yield-per-dollar** vs other topics (Fig 11 right, mentioned explicitly).
- Crypto appears in the set with highest profit outliers per condition.
- **Short-window (5m/15m) crypto up/down markets are NOT separately analyzed** — the paper covers the Polymarket universe broadly, not our specific market type.

### Duration / Persistence

- Paper does not report exact duration of arb windows in seconds/blocks explicitly.
- The 950-block grouping window (≈ 1 hour) suggests opportunities can persist for tens of minutes.
- From Fig 10: ~1% of estimated US election opportunities were exploited → **99% of opportunities go uncaptured**, indicating low competition in most markets.
- "Arbitrage happens during periods of volatility" — paper uses single-block averaging to catch short-lived windows.

## 5. Detection Methodology (Brief)

Paper's heuristic reduction:
1. **Temporal filter**: Markets must share same end date / same event.
2. **Topic clustering**: Textual embeddings (Linq-Embed-Mistral) → 7 topic buckets.
3. **LLM dependency checker**: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B checks pairwise market semantic dependency. Checks: (i) valid JSON response, (ii) exactly one TRUE per market, (iii) output size ≤ n+m.
4. False positive handling: Markets reduced to ≤4 conditions (top by liquidity) + 5th "other" OR-condition. >90% of liquidity in top 4 conditions.
5. Validation: 374 candidate pairs → 13 confirmed Combinatorial Arb pairs.

**Not relevant for crypto up/down markets** — our markets are single-condition binary, no inter-market combinatorial dependency to detect.

## 6. Sizing and EV Formulas

**Paper provides no optimal sizing formula.** The profit calculation used is:

```
profit = min_tokens_held × (1 − cost_to_acquire_min_tokens)
```

Where `min_tokens_held` = minimum tokens held across rolling position window (pair-matched quantity), and cost = weighted average acquisition price.

**Fees**: Paper explicitly states "we do not take into account fees because Polymarket currently does not charge per trade executed." This reflects the measurement period (pre-fee change). **Our current framework must apply the 0.07 × p × (1−p) winner-only taker fee** introduced later, which the paper does not model.

**No coverage of**:
- Queue position effects on fill probability
- Optimal ladder spacing under partial fills
- EV under fee curve given spread level
- Sizing relative to available liquidity

For sizing guidance, our own simulation work (`_maker_queue_bt.py`) is more relevant than this paper.

## 7. What Changes for Our Ladder-Bot Replication

### Confirmed by paper (validates our framework)

1. **sum_ask < 1 is the correct formal gate for long MRA**. Paper's Definition 3 Long = our filter. No correction needed.
2. **sum_bid > 1 is symmetric short MRA**. Paper shows short strategy (Split → sell YES) is viable. Per Fig 16 (Appendix H): "overall shorting has more profit" in appendix analysis. This direction (sum_bid > 1, maker sell-side) may be underexplored in our own work.
3. **Non-atomic execution risk is fundamental** — not a bug in our approach, but a stated property of the CLOB. The ladder approach (GTC orders waiting for takers) is implicitly handling this by making the fill dependent on taker aggression, not our own two-leg sequencing.
4. **No per-trade fee at measurement time** — since then Polymarket introduced the 0.07 curve (taker only, winner-only). Our existing fee model is correct but the paper's profit estimates are overstated vs current conditions. The $39.6M figure ignores fees.

### Insights that change our approach

5. **Short (sell-side) arb may be larger than long** in multi-condition markets. In binary up/down specifically, shorting = selling both tokens when sum_bid > 1. We have NOT systematically tracked sum_bid > 1 windows. This is a gap worth measuring — if the up/down book regularly has sum_bid > 1 (both best bids sum > $1), a maker who posts on the buy side waiting for this could capture it. **However**: our b945 chain analysis shows only paired taker fills (he buys at ask, not rests on bid catching takers). The sell-side path is not yet in our live stack.
6. **Buying NO dominates in multi-condition NegRisk markets** ($17.3M). Not directly applicable to binary, but confirms the market inefficiency direction: NO tokens are frequently underpriced. In binary crypto: the "NO" of UP = "DN", and the "NO" of DN = "UP" — it's symmetric. The asymmetry is in which side is cheaper when sum_ask < 1. Our pair-arb logic already captures both sides.
7. **Top extractors are NOT crypto up/down specialists**. The $2M top wallet has 4,049 transactions vs b945's >250k fills. The paper's top-10 are likely election/sports one-shot sweepers. **Crypto up/down arb is a DIFFERENT competitive cohort, not visible in this paper's top-10**. Our competition is other crypto micro-arb bots not yet profiled.
8. **Arb windows persist for 5K blocks (≈ 2.5 hours) in the worst case** (price carry-forward limit). In practice, election opportunities were only 1% exploited — very low competition. For crypto up/down (higher frequency, more liquidity), competition should be higher but the paper gives no data on 5m/15m window specifically.
9. **Paper does not model maker vs taker execution**. All profits computed from bid data (taker-equivalent perspective). The MAKER rebate path ($0 fee + rebate income) that b945 uses for ~36% of his net is entirely invisible to this paper. **Our b945 edge is partially outside the paper's modeling scope**.
10. **Fee model gap is critical**: Paper assumes zero fees. At our current fee curve (0.07 × p × (1−p)), for p=0.50 max fee = $0.0175/share. On a $1 position at sum_ask=0.97 (3 cent edge), the taker fee costs ~$0.0175 × 2 legs (UP fill + DN fill) = ~$0.035 per $1 — consuming ~1.2% of the 3-cent edge. At sum_ask=0.94 (6 cent edge), fee burden is smaller relatively. **Confirmed: the sum_ask < ~0.97 gate from our prior work is roughly where fees eat all edge, consistent with the paper's implicit assumption of positive arb existing at sum < 1**.

### Implication for Rust replication

- **Two-sided simultaneous GTC posting** (one on each binary leg) is the correct architecture. Paper confirms non-atomic execution is the fundamental risk; being a maker (already posted) removes the need to atomically sweep both legs.
- **Monitor sum_bid > 1** (short arb) in addition to sum_ask < 1 (long arb). If the bid side of UP + bid side of DN > 1, placing limit SELL orders on both legs is a valid short strategy.
- **NegRisk CONVERSION is irrelevant** for our binary crypto up/down markets — these are single-condition CTF contracts, not NegRisk multi-outcome.
- **No gas cost concern** — Polymarket gasless execution means per-trade on-chain cost is borne by Polymarket operators.
